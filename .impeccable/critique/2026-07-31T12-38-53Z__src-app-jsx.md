## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Nudge feels authored rather than category-interchangeable. Its Clear Signal system—ink and white planes, signal yellow, hard edges, condensed type, oversized actions—forms a coherent identity without streaks, trophies, or motivational theater. The ready and active-set states embody the product especially well: current work, the next action, and available alternatives are immediately legible. The main missed opportunity is tonal: extreme scale and exhaustive accounting sometimes drift toward a generic high-intensity gym aesthetic instead of Nudge’s calmer promise that training fits real life.

## Overall Impression

The core Perform experience now has real conviction: one obvious action, large phone-friendly controls, and alternatives that preserve agency without asking the user to re-plan. The weakest moments sit around that peak. Plan delays its action behind optional choices, Cooldown continues to advertise more work, and Review frames a legitimate partial session as a deficit. The biggest opportunity is to make the beginning and ending as focused and nonjudgmental as the active set.

## What’s Working

- **The active-set hierarchy is excellent.** The oversized Start set action is easy to hit while handling weights, and the surrounding exercise list stays available without competing at equal weight.
- **The visual system is coherent and product-specific.** Signal yellow consistently means “the next useful action,” while hard-edged black, white, and concrete surfaces make state changes unmistakable.
- **Agency is preserved.** Users can jump exercises, edit results, cancel active work, resume from Cooldown, or finish early without authoritative coaching or guilt language.

## Priority Issues

### 1. [P1] Cooldown presents two incompatible primary intentions

**Why it matters:** The phase says Cooldown, but a yellow Start set remains above the Finish action and eight exercise choices remain exposed. A tired phone user must decide whether Nudge wants them to stop or continue.

**Fix:** Put the yellow Finish workout action immediately after the Cooldown status. Keep Resume workout secondary beside or below it, and collapse unfinished exercise controls under a neutral “Return to remaining work” disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### 2. [P1] Review emphasizes the deficit and saving has no acknowledged ending

**Why it matters:** “1 of 25 items confirmed,” seven zero-result rows, and a silent return to Plan make a valid short session feel incomplete and leave the user unsure whether it saved. That conflicts directly with “Training fits real life.”

**Fix:** Lead with factual completed work and duration, such as “1 set recorded · 1:28.” Collapse unconfirmed planned work into one disclosure. After Save, focus and announce a factual “Workout saved” state before presenting the next Plan action.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### 3. [P2] Plan hides its dominant action behind optional configuration

**Why it matters:** At 390×844, the large question, time slider, and seven recovery toggles push Plan my workout below the first viewport. Optional recovery choices become the main visual task and return planning work to the user.

**Fix:** Keep time and Plan my workout in the first task-sized chunk. Move “Anything to work around?” into an optional disclosure, or restore a compact two-column selection layout if all choices must remain visible.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### 4. [P2] The “Performance” heading overflows at the target mobile width

**Why it matters:** The live detector measured a 20px overflow at 390px. It creates a cramped header beside the timer and undermines an otherwise precise responsive system.

**Fix:** Give the heading/timer grid more room at narrow widths or reduce the Performance heading’s mobile size until it remains inside its box without weakening the phase hierarchy.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### 5. [P2] Elapsed-day confirmation prompts reintroduce pressure

**Why it matters:** Blocking prompts such as “It has been N days since your last leg workout” interrupt the authored journey and can make irregular schedules sound like neglect, even though Nudge promises to adapt without guilt.

**Fix:** Preserve the existing choice and generation semantics, but present it as neutral inline context without elapsed-day framing or a blocking browser confirm.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Cognitive Load

The journey has **three checklist failures**—single focus, chunking, and minimal visible choices—so cognitive load is moderate and should be addressed before release. Grouping, visual hierarchy during active work, working-memory support, and progressive disclosure for prior sets are strong.

Decision points exceeding four visible options:

- Plan recovery selection: **7** muscle-group toggles.
- Ready/Perform exercise jump: **8** exercise choices.
- Cooldown: **11** obvious choices—8 exercises, Start set, Resume workout, and Finish workout.
- Review has only two actions, but its **8-row** result breakdown exceeds the four-item information chunk guideline.

## Emotional Journey

Plan feels decisive in tone but visually asks too much before revealing its action. Workout Ready and active Performance are the peak: Nudge clearly carries the plan while leaving alternatives available. Active rest stays legible and action-oriented. Cooldown breaks the transition by continuing to promote work. Review is the emotional valley because its accounting emphasizes what was not completed. The silent post-save return then weakens the peak-end effect and confidence in persistence.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** “recommendation,” “met the floor,” and confirmed-item accounting assume training or product knowledge. Review can read as failure, and Save provides no visible confirmation.
- **Sam, accessibility-dependent:** Native controls, labels, live status, focus movement, and reduced-motion support are strong. The long linear tab order through eight exercise disclosures and the distant Cooldown actions remain costly.
- **Casey, distracted mobile:** Touch targets are excellent, but repeated scrolling, the top-heavy Plan screen, and the silent post-save return increase interruption risk.
- **Morgan, irregular-schedule trainee:** The elapsed-day prompt and deficit-focused Review undermine the promise that whatever useful work fit today was legitimate.

## Minor Observations

- Manage Catalog remains prominent during active training and competes with session focus.
- Second-level precision across actual and planned phase times makes Review feel more forensic than useful.
- The emulator-only warning obstructs the bottom viewport in test evidence but is not production UI.
- The live overlay’s cream-palette warning conflicts with the approved design brief and should remain ignored for this surface.

## Questions to Consider

- If training fits real life, why does an early finish end with “1 of 25” instead of “1 set recorded”?
- During Cooldown, is the user cooling down or still performing? The current hierarchy answers both.
- Can Nudge create signal through order and proximity more often, reserving enormous typography for the one action that truly needs it?
