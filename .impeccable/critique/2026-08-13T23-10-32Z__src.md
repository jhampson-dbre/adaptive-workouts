## Priority Issues

### 1. [P2] The dominant planning action precedes the optional recovery input

**Why it matters:** `Plan my workout` is visually and structurally before `Any areas need rest?`. A user can commit without noticing an input that may materially change the generated workout, even though “none” is a reasonable default.

**Fix:** Move the recovery disclosure before the CTA or expose a persistent summary such as `Areas resting: none`. Keep progressive disclosure, but let the single yellow action follow all planning inputs.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify src/components/Generator.jsx`

### 2. [P2] Mobile shell chrome still consumes too much of the action frame

**Why it matters:** In the in-app harness, the requested 390×844 review rendered as a 293×634 CSS viewport; the two-row global navigation and journey rail pushed the planning CTA below that effective first viewport. The exact 390×844 CSS behavior remains unconfirmed, but the narrow-layout pressure is visible.

**Fix:** Demote Sign out into Settings or an account disclosure and test a compact mobile navigation treatment that preserves labels, active state, and 44px targets.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt src/App.jsx`

### 3. [P2] Advanced Settings remains dense and under-explained

**Why it matters:** Four job links, catalog filters, 15 exercise rows, repeated actions, and vocabulary such as Tier 1, Tier 3, tracking modes, and saved orders make Settings feel like a maintenance console. Beginners can act without understanding consequences; keyboard and touch users traverse many repeated controls.

**Fix:** Reveal one Settings job at a time, keep search/filter ahead of catalog results, collapse secondary row metadata, and add concise task-focused explanations beside advanced terms.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill src/components/Settings.jsx`
