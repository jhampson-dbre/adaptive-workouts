## Overall Impression

The active workout is the product at its best: decisive, accessible, calm, and physically usable. The biggest opportunity is to extend that same “one clear signal” discipline to the moments before, after, and outside a workout—especially generated ordering, History, Settings, and save completion.

## What's Working

- The active-workout visual identity is distinctive and coherent; phase, timer, current set, and next action are immediately legible.
- Large targets, semantic labels, visible focus, status messaging, and reversible transitions show serious accessibility intent.
- Early finish is unusually humane: it states consequences plainly, preserves agency, and avoids guilt or gamification.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Saved completion and History contradict each other

**Why it matters:** After Save workout, the app confirmed “Workout saved” and “This workout is complete,” while the already-expanded History still said “No workouts logged yet” after another 1.2 seconds. Users cannot tell whether their workout was actually preserved.

**Fix:** Make successful save update or invalidate the visible History state atomically. Keep the factual completion summary until History reflects the saved entry; if refresh fails, show an explicit retry rather than contradictory success and emptiness.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Primary actions sit below long prerequisite-looking content

**Why it matters:** At 1440×900, Plan my workout was clipped at the bottom. On generated Workout ready, both desktop and phone required scrolling past eight order rows and repeated move controls before Start workout. The interface makes optional inspection feel mandatory and drains momentum at the exact commitment point.

**Fix:** Keep the dominant action in the first decision viewport. Put Start workout directly beneath the generated summary and move ordering into a neutral “Review or change order” disclosure. Preserve the existing accessible reorder controls inside it.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Expanded History is a forensic data wall

**Why it matters:** One partial workout expanded into eight exercise sections and 25 set records, repeating “Not confirmed” and prerequisite copy. Users looking to remember the workout must parse implementation-level detail.

**Fix:** Lead with duration, confirmed work, omissions, and per-exercise completion summaries. Disclose individual set detail separately for each exercise.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Settings combines three tools in one uninterrupted page

**Why it matters:** Defaults, supersets, a seven-field add form, and 15 catalog rows with 30 row actions share one long mobile page. Inline editing expands roughly ten more fields, making resumption after interruption costly.

**Fix:** Keep the route and behavior, but add a compact section index or disclosures for Defaults, Supersets, and Catalog. Open one editor at a time and visually quiet surrounding rows; add catalog search only if real catalog growth warrants it.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] The end state and navigation vocabulary lose clarity

**Why it matters:** “Manage Catalog” opens “Settings,” which offers both “Back to Generator” and “Close.” After completion, Review’s useful facts disappear into a thin “This workout is complete” state, weakening the peak-end experience.

**Fix:** Name the shell action Settings, retain one consistent exit such as Back to plan, and keep a compact factual completion receipt with duration, confirmed sets, and “Saved to workout history.”

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey — Distracted Mobile User:** Starting a generated workout requires scrolling through the entire exercise order; long History and Catalog pages are hard to resume after interruption. Perform and rest are strong counterexamples: large thumb targets, preserved context, visible timing, and one dominant action.

**Sam — Accessibility-Dependent User:** Semantic headings, named controls, status regions, programmatic focus, and 4px focus treatment are strong. The remaining cost is linear: up to 15 reorder actions and 30 catalog row actions before form controls. Condensed progress labels and dense metadata may also fatigue low-vision users; 200% reflow was not tested.

**Interrupted gym-floor trainee:** The next set or recovery action must be instantly recoverable while handling equipment. Perform/rest meets that need; generated ordering does not. The early-finish inventory can also push its confirmation controls below the phone viewport at an already stressful moment.

## Cognitive Load

The live review failed six checklist areas on dense surfaces: single focus, chunking, visual hierarchy, one thing at a time, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. Grouping and working-memory support generally pass.

Decision points above four visible options include seven Plan constraints; eight generated exercises with up to sixteen reorder controls; eight active-workout disclosures; seven Add exercise fields; roughly ten inline weighted-edit fields; 15 catalog rows with 30 row actions; and 25 set records in one expanded History entry.

## Emotional Journey

Plan is calm and agency-preserving. Workout ready is the primary valley because ordering stalls momentum. Perform/rest is the peak: current work, time, and the yellow next action are unmistakable. Early finish reassures without judgment, and Review feels trustworthy. Completion is too abrupt and, in the desktop run, contradicted History—an especially damaging end-state because users leave unsure whether their work was saved.

## Minor Observations

- The blue focus rectangle on programmatically focused headings orients well but can briefly resemble editable selection.
- Progress labels are visually small relative to the otherwise generous type scale.
- “Starting recommendation” may imply more intelligence than a stored setting/history value supports.
- Catalog rows remain readable and well separated despite their length.
- Cooldown has the cleanest hierarchy in the application.

## Questions to Consider

- Is exercise reordering truly part of every workout start, or should generated order be accepted by default with editing as an exception?
- Should History optimize for remembering what happened or for forensic inspection of every planned and unconfirmed set?
- Can Settings remain one route while behaving like three focused tools: defaults, supersets, and catalog?
- What factual accomplishment should remain visible at the end without crossing into praise or gamification?
